# 222 Emails Facebook Groups Acquisition OS

## Mission
Turn relevant Facebook groups into a repeatable source of qualified UK owner conversations and Revenue Leak Audit opportunities without spam, browser automation or unnecessary infrastructure.

## System boundary
GitHub is the lightweight control plane. Facebook is the human execution surface. The existing 222 Emails audit pipeline remains the sales pipeline.

Do not build a second CRM, standalone dashboard, browser bot, Netlify app or automated DM sender for this experiment.

## Non-negotiables
1. GitHub controls daily planning, targeting, QA prompts and review cadence.
2. The final Facebook group post is always made manually.
3. Check the group's live rules immediately before every post.
4. Respect each group's configured cooling period.
5. Never paste identical copy into multiple groups back-to-back.
6. No cold DM to group members solely because they are members. DM after a relevant public interaction, explicit invitation or a legitimate prospecting basis.
7. Direct promotion is reserved for groups where current rules permit it. In owner and vertical communities use authority-first diagnostics.
8. Every qualified lead goes into the existing 222 Emails audit pipeline.
9. Exact post copy lives in `POSTS.md`.
10. Maximum primary volume is 3 group posts per day.

## 30-day operating cadence
- Days 1 to 7: validate group rules, hooks and owner response.
- Days 8 to 14: concentrate on groups producing comments, URLs and audit requests.
- Days 15 to 21: retire weak groups and increase useful mini-audit activity in strong groups.
- Days 22 to 30: allocate most effort to groups and archetypes producing qualified audits, offers or revenue.

## Daily operating loop
1. Open the GitHub issue titled `[FB GROUPS DAY XX]`.
2. Check the three selected groups and their current rules.
3. Check the configured cooling period.
4. Open `POSTS.md` and use the named archetype.
5. Apply the smallest relevant group or segment adaptation.
6. Post manually.
7. Paste each Facebook post URL into the GitHub issue.
8. Spend 10 to 15 minutes answering genuine comments.
9. Move explicit audit requests into DM.
10. Log every requested audit in the existing 222 Emails audit pipeline and add the Facebook source data.
11. Complete the GitHub issue scorecard and close it when follow-up is captured.

## Comment scripts
### CTA response
Thanks [Name]. Send me your website/store URL by DM and I’ll take a look. I’ll focus on the highest-impact customer-journey leak I can identify first, then tell you what I would prioritise.

### What does the audit include?
I look at the journey from first visit or enquiry through conversion, onboarding, repeat purchase and reactivation. The goal is not to hand you a generic email checklist. It is to identify where people are currently dropping out and what follow-up system could realistically recover more of that revenue.

### We already use email marketing
That is usually where the interesting part starts. Having email running and having the right lifecycle system are different things. I’d look at what is missing, where sequencing is weak, whether customer behaviour changes the messaging, and where existing traffic or customers are not being followed up properly.

### Price question
The audit itself is free. If there is a worthwhile gap and you want 222 Emails to implement it afterwards, I would scope that separately. There is no obligation to buy anything from the audit.

## Lead stages
NEW_ENGAGEMENT -> QUALIFIED -> URL_RECEIVED -> AUDIT_QUEUED -> AUDIT_DELIVERED -> IMPLEMENTATION_OFFERED -> WON / NURTURE / LOST

## Qualification
Prioritise businesses with existing traffic, customers, enquiries, bookings or an active ecommerce store. Strong signals include paid acquisition, repeat-purchase potential, an existing customer list, obvious lifecycle gaps and a decision-maker engaging directly.

## MVP KPIs
### Activity guardrails
- Maximum 3 high-quality group posts per day
- 0 rule-violating posts
- 0 identical mass-post bursts
- 100% meaningful comments answered
- 100% requested audits logged

### Week 1 validation target
- 15 to 20 compliant posts
- 10+ meaningful owner conversations
- 5+ audit requests
- 3+ qualified audits

### Week 2 validation target
- 15 to 20 compliant posts
- 15+ meaningful owner conversations
- 7+ audit requests
- 5+ qualified audits
- 2+ implementation conversations

### Channel validation threshold by Day 30
A strong validation result is approximately:
- 60 to 80 compliant posts
- 20+ meaningful owner conversations
- 10+ genuine audit requests
- 5+ qualified audits
- 2+ paid opportunities
- 1+ customer

The primary optimisation metric is qualified audits per 10 posts. Secondary metrics are comment-to-DM rate, DM-to-URL rate, URL-to-audit rate, audit-to-offer rate, offer-to-win rate, revenue per group and revenue per archetype.

Do not optimise for impressions alone.

## Scale, watch, retire
- SCALE: 2+ qualified leads from a group within 10 posts, or any won client attributable to the group.
- WATCH: meaningful engagement but no qualified lead after 5 compliant posts.
- RETIRE: 10 compliant posts with no qualified conversations, repeated moderation friction or an audience dominated by sellers rather than buyers.
- PAUSE: immediately if admins warn against promotion or posts are repeatedly removed.

## Omega review every 7 days
Score each group and archetype on:
1. ICP density
2. rule compatibility
3. meaningful comments
4. qualified leads
5. audits requested
6. offers created
7. revenue won
8. time cost
9. moderation risk
10. repeatability

Reallocate the next week's posting slots toward the top performers. Never increase volume to compensate for weak relevance.

## When to build the full Acquisition OS
Only expand the infrastructure after the channel proves it can produce qualified opportunities. A useful trigger is either:
- one customer won from Facebook Groups, or
- two or more implementation opportunities with clear source attribution.

Until then, keep the system deliberately lean.

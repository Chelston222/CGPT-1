DENTAL PRACTICE · WEB EDITION

Client Return, Made Simple.

Use this with the practice-management system, recall list and enquiry workflow open. The objective is operational clarity around practice-approved next steps, not automating clinical judgement.

Core rule: do not add automation to a process you cannot explain on one page. Define the trigger, eligible person, next action, owner, stop condition and measurement first.

01 · DIAGNOSE

The six places value most often disappears.

The leak is not “manual follow-up” by default. It is the moment where the next commercially useful step becomes fragile, unclear or unowned.

03 · BUILD

Six systems. Fix one before you build six.

01

Enquiry follow-up

Moment: A suitable prospective patient asks but does not book.

System job: Define the next operational action without diagnosing or recommending treatment.

02

Recall follow-up

Moment: A practice-defined recall state becomes due or overdue.

System job: Use practice-approved timing and stop as soon as the state changes.

03

Missed-appointment recovery

Moment: A booked appointment is cancelled or missed.

System job: Create a respectful operational route back.

04

Treatment-plan progression

Moment: An approved next action is waiting.

System job: Assign operational ownership and escalate clinical questions.

05

Human handoff

Moment: A reply needs judgement.

System job: Move it to the right member of the practice team rather than forcing automation.

06

Dormant-patient return

Moment: An eligible former patient is inactive.

System job: Apply permissions, relevance and practice policy before any marketing activity.

Priority rule: pick the leak with the clearest commercial value, enough eligible volume, a workable next action and a result you can actually measure.

04 · MESSAGE

Re-open the decision. Do not manufacture pressure.

These are frameworks, not copy-and-paste spam. Replace brackets with genuine context and make the message fit the relationship, channel and permission state.

Enquiry follow-up

You contacted the practice about [enquiry topic]. If you still want help arranging the appropriate next step, reply here and the team can assist.

Missed appointment

We noticed your appointment did not go ahead. If you would like help arranging another suitable time, please reply or use [booking route].

Recall operational prompt

According to the practice’s records, you may be due to arrange your next appointment. If you would like help with booking, here are the options: [route].

05 · 14-DAY TEST

Run a controlled recovery process, not a database blast.

Days 1–3Clean, suppress, segment and define the single objective.
Days 4–6Build the message, booking/enrolment route and reply ownership.
Days 7–10Launch to the eligible cohort and follow up proportionately.
Days 11–14Reconcile replies, conversions, completed value and learning.

Success is not an open rate. Useful outcomes are qualified replies, appropriate next steps, bookings or enrolments where evidenced, completed value where measurable, objections and operational friction.

06 · MEASURE

Use a small dashboard tied to decisions.

Conversion

Define the cohort, event and time window. Compare like with like.

Recovered value

Track booked/committed value separately from completed or realised revenue.

Learning

Record objections, non-response, reply ownership and process failures, not just campaign metrics.

07 · GUARDRAILS

Useful systems stay inside the business’s real authority.

222Emails does not set recall intervals, diagnose, recommend treatment or decide patient suitability. Those decisions remain with the dental practice and qualified professionals. Patient data may be sensitive; UK GDPR, PECR and practice policy must be applied before outreach.

This guide is operational guidance, not legal, clinical, financial or regulatory advice. Where the lawful basis, clinical boundary or sector rule is unclear, check the relevant regulator or obtain appropriate professional advice before sending.

SALON · WEB EDITION

Client Return, Made Simple.

Use this with your diary and booking history open. The objective is to stop good clients quietly drifting out of the return journey because the next step depends on memory.

Core rule: do not add automation to a process you cannot explain on one page. Define the trigger, eligible person, next action, owner, stop condition and measurement first.

01 · DIAGNOSE

The six places value most often disappears.

The leak is not “manual follow-up” by default. It is the moment where the next commercially useful step becomes fragile, unclear or unowned.

03 · BUILD

Six systems. Fix one before you build six.

01

First-to-second visit

Moment: A new client has a good first service but no next appointment.

System job: Create a clear route to the second visit without turning checkout into pressure.

02

Rebooking

Moment: A client leaves without another booking.

System job: Make the next appointment easier to plan at the appropriate moment.

03

Due-back

Moment: The normal service cadence approaches.

System job: Use booking history and business-defined timing rather than random campaigns.

04

Cancellation recovery

Moment: A chair becomes empty after a cancellation or no-show.

System job: Give the interrupted booking a defined route back.

05

Lapsed regular return

Moment: A reliable pattern breaks.

System job: Detect the change and re-open the relationship appropriately.

06

Quiet-capacity reactivation

Moment: The salon has genuine capacity.

System job: Prioritise suitable existing clients before defaulting to more acquisition spend.

Priority rule: pick the leak with the clearest commercial value, enough eligible volume, a workable next action and a result you can actually measure.

04 · MESSAGE

Re-open the decision. Do not manufacture pressure.

These are frameworks, not copy-and-paste spam. Replace brackets with genuine context and make the message fit the relationship, channel and permission state.

Due-back

It has been around [time] since your last [service]. If you were planning your next one, here is the easiest way to find a time that suits you: [route].

Lapsed regular

We have not seen you for a while and wanted to check in. If you are still keeping on top of [service], we would be happy to help you find a suitable next appointment.

Cancellation

No problem about [appointment]. Would you like us to help find another time, or would you prefer to leave it for now?

05 · 14-DAY TEST

Run a controlled recovery process, not a database blast.

Days 1–3Clean, suppress, segment and define the single objective.
Days 4–6Build the message, booking/enrolment route and reply ownership.
Days 7–10Launch to the eligible cohort and follow up proportionately.
Days 11–14Reconcile replies, conversions, completed value and learning.

Success is not an open rate. Useful outcomes are qualified replies, appropriate next steps, bookings or enrolments where evidenced, completed value where measurable, objections and operational friction.

06 · MEASURE

Use a small dashboard tied to decisions.

Conversion

Define the cohort, event and time window. Compare like with like.

Recovered value

Track booked/committed value separately from completed or realised revenue.

Learning

Record objections, non-response, reply ownership and process failures, not just campaign metrics.

07 · GUARDRAILS

Useful systems stay inside the business’s real authority.

Return windows should come from the salon’s own service model, history and customer expectations. Marketing permissions and opt-outs still matter. Do not turn a healthy client relationship into constant promotional pressure.

08 · 30-DAY PLAN

Turn the diagnosis into an operating rhythm.

Week 1Define states, ownership, eligibility and baseline numbers.
Week 2Build the highest-priority next-step journey.
Week 3Run a controlled recovery or return test.
Week 4Reconcile outcomes, document learning and decide what becomes ongoing.

TEST BEFORE A FULL REBUILD

One eligible segment. One controlled campaign. Fourteen days of real behaviour.

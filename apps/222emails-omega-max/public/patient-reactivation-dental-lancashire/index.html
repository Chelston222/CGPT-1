PRACTICE-APPROVED LOGIC

Clinical decisions stay with the dental team. The system organises the operational follow-up around them.

222Emails does not set clinical recall intervals, recommend treatment or decide patient suitability. The practice supplies the approved rules. The Client Return System defines triggers, communication, suppression, ownership and stop conditions around those rules.

Status setThe practice defines the relevant patient or appointment state.
EligibleConsent and practice rules determine whether follow-up is appropriate.
Follow-upThe right channel or staff action is triggered.
ResponseReplies and exceptions move to human ownership when required.
Booked / closedAutomation stops or reroutes when the state changes.

BOUNDARIES THAT MATTER

Automation should organise approved follow-up, not imitate a clinician.

Appropriate system jobs

  • Identify a practice-defined follow-up state
  • Apply permission and suppression rules
  • Send or queue an approved operational message
  • Escalate replies that need staff judgement
  • Stop when an appointment is booked or state changes

Human or clinical jobs

  • Diagnosing or recommending treatment
  • Setting patient-specific clinical intervals
  • Resolving clinical questions through generic automation
  • Determining suitability without the dental team
  • Making unsupported outcome claims

WHY A SYSTEM BEATS A ONE-OFF BLAST

Reactivation creates more value when the patient journey stays organised after the campaign ends.

A one-off message can create bookings. A Client Return System also defines what happens after future recalls, cancellations, enquiries, replies and staff handoffs, so the practice is not rebuilding the same process repeatedly.

DENTAL QUESTIONS

Operational clarity without crossing clinical lines.

Do you set patient recall intervals?

No. Recall timing and clinical decisions belong to the dental practice and qualified professionals. 222Emails builds workflow around practice-approved rules.

Can old patients simply be emailed?

Not automatically. Consent, lawful basis, suppression, relevance and practice policy need to be considered before any reactivation work. A database entry alone does not remove those requirements.

Do we need a new practice-management system?

Not by default. We first assess whether the current systems can expose the required patient states and support the operational actions safely.

Can this replace reception follow-up?

It can reduce repetitive manual chasing where appropriate, but important replies, exceptions and clinical matters remain human-owned.

# Triple Two Emails LinkedIn content rules

## Brand naming

- Public-facing brand name: **Triple Two Emails**.
- Do not use founder-origin filler such as “this is why I made 222 Emails”, “this is why I built 222 Emails”, “this is why I started 222 Emails”, or equivalent phrasing.
- When referring to the company from Chelston’s personal LinkedIn, prefer a native LinkedIn Page mention of **Triple Two Emails** when the publishing route has verified annotation metadata.
- Never fake a native mention by assuming plain `@Triple Two Emails` will become clickable. If native annotation metadata is unavailable, use plain **Triple Two Emails** instead.
- On posts published by the Triple Two Emails Page itself, do not awkwardly self-tag the Page unless there is a specific editorial reason.

## Voice

- UK English.
- No em dashes.
- Outcome first: retention, repeat revenue, client return systems, missed-revenue recovery and lifecycle revenue.
- Avoid generic agency-origin stories unless the founder story itself is the subject of the post.
- Do not invent metrics, customer results, testimonials or certainty.
- Write with a clear point of view. Prefer direct statements over hedged, generic motivational language.
- The copy must sound specific to Chelston, the actual image, the actual business or the actual moment. If the same caption could plausibly sit under almost any founder photo, reject and rewrite it.
- Do not manufacture drama. Conviction must come from a real belief, decision, observation, result, trade-off or lived detail.

## Founder and image-post standard

- Scheduled founder and image posts should normally target **700 to 1,500 characters**. Treat **600 characters** as the normal floor unless the post is deliberately approved as a short-form or micro post.
- Put tension, a clear belief, a surprising observation or a concrete scene in the opening two lines. Do not spend the opening on throat-clearing.
- Include at least one concrete detail that belongs to the specific image or moment.
- Develop one central idea rather than stacking generic lessons.
- Make the implication explicit: what does this moment change, prove, reinforce or reveal about how Chelston is building, operating, selling, learning or living?
- Use stronger declarative language where the evidence supports it. Avoid repeated softeners such as “maybe”, “sometimes”, “I guess”, “I think” or “kind of”.
- Avoid repetitive founder clichés such as “the next level”, “keep grinding”, “the journey”, “work hard”, “trust the process” and interchangeable success motivation unless the phrasing is genuinely specific and earned.
- End on a line with consequence, tension, commitment, contrast or a memorable thought. Do not default to generic closers such as “hope everyone has a good week”.
- A founder post does not need a forced Triple Two Emails plug, but it should strengthen the reader’s understanding of the person building the company.
- Before approval, run the **spark test**: would a reader recognise a distinct voice, a real point of view and a reason to remember the post after scrolling away? If not, rewrite it.

## Queue reserve

- Automatic replenishment stops at **8 scheduled placements per channel**.
- The Buffer ceiling remains **10** so that **2 slots stay reserved** for timely, owner-requested or in-the-moment content.
- Automatic refill must never consume slots 9 or 10.
- If a timely post temporarily takes occupancy above 8, do not replenish again until occupancy falls below 8.
- Never sacrifice content quality merely to restore the queue to 8. A weaker queue is preferable to filling the reserve with sub-standard posts.

## Publishing safety

- Content reservoir or QA-ready status is not publishing permission.
- Only the owner-approved GitHub publishing gate may release content to Buffer.
- Native LinkedIn mentions must use verified LinkedIn entity metadata when sent through the Buffer API.

# LinkedIn Story Worth Reading Standard v1.0

Effective: 20 September 2026
Status: CURRENT CANONICAL PERSONAL-NARRATIVE STANDARD
Applies to: Chelston personal LinkedIn founder posts, photo-led posts and narrative companion copy

## Governing idea

**Start with what actually happened. Make the reader care before trying to teach them anything.**

A photograph is context, evidence or atmosphere. It is not automatically the story.

The story is the real event, tension, contradiction, number, admission, decision, failure, trade-off or unresolved stake behind it.

## Story selection hierarchy

Use this order:

1. Real current operating tension.
2. Specific event or scene.
3. Concrete number, contradiction, admission, decision or failure.
4. What changed or remains unproven.
5. Only then, the image or asset that supports the story.

If one missing fact is the difference between a surface caption and a real story, ask Chelston **one precise question**. Never invent the scene, feeling, result, timeline or stakes.

## Hook rule

The first one or two visible lines should normally carry one of:

- a real event;
- a meaningful number;
- a contradiction;
- an admission;
- a failure or mistake;
- a decision with consequences;
- an unresolved stake.

The hook must create a genuine reason to continue, not merely sound punchy.

A useful structural example is:

**“I’m trying to make £2.5k weeks boring.”**

The value is not in repeating that wording. The mechanism is:

**current truth + specificity + tension + unfinished business**

One good week is not the story. Building repeatability is.

Do not force the £2.5k figure into unrelated posts and do not turn it into a slogan.

## Narrative movement

Preferred story spine:

**HOOK → SCENE → FRICTION → WHAT HAPPENED → CURRENT TRUTH → EARNED ENDING**

This is a guide, not a template.

A real story should contain movement. Something happens, changes, fails, conflicts, becomes clearer, remains unresolved or costs something.

If nothing materially happens, there probably is not a story yet.

## Narrative architecture selector

Canonical child layer: `docs/LINKEDIN_NARRATIVE_ARCHITECTURE_OS.md`.

Only choose a framework after source truth and Story Worth Reading have passed. Frameworks are optional. A real story may use RAW STORY, SLAY OVERLAY, CONTRAST / GAP, FAILURE / CORRECTION, OPERATOR / BUILD-IN-PUBLIC or another truthful shape. Teaching and POV content may use their own non-story architectures.

Lara Acosta's public SLAY framework is retained with attribution as an optional overlay: **Story → Lesson → Actionable Advice → You**. It is not the default and must not force a lesson, list, question or tidy resolution onto a story that does not earn one.

Kleo is retained only as a creator-research-method benchmark: study structure, relative outliers and connection mechanisms; transfer the mechanism to Chelston's facts; never clone wording, persona, cadence or templates.

If the framework is more visible than the story, rewrite.

## Current-truth rule

The strongest founder content usually lives in the gap between:

- what Chelston wants;
- what is true today;
- what he is doing about it;
- what is still not proven.

Do not write from an imagined future identity.

Do not make a one-off result sound repeatable.

Do not turn a live experiment into a retrospective lesson before the outcome exists.

“I do not know yet”, “this did not work”, “I was wrong” and “we still have to prove this repeats” are valid endings when true.

## Aspiration without envy bait

Cars, travel, money, lifestyle and success imagery must never borrow status the business has not earned.

If an aspirational image is used, anchor the story to a real current gap, operating target, responsibility, trade-off or unfinished piece of work.

The current reality is more interesting than pretending the future has already arrived.

## Hard reject list

Reject or rewrite:

- image descriptions masquerading as stories;
- generic founder motivation;
- envy bait or success cosplay;
- polished hindsight that removes the mess;
- fake vulnerability or manufactured confession;
- “one thing I learned” openings used as filler;
- tidy three-part morals created because they sound complete;
- meta-posts about posting, authenticity or personal branding unless that is genuinely the event;
- visible trivia such as walls, lighting, outfit, pose, meal or number of selfies when it does not materially change what happened;
- generic endings that could attach to any founder post;
- copy that could swap Chelston’s name for another founder with no meaningful change;
- padding added only to make the caption longer.

## Five release tests

Before approval:

1. **Random-founder test:** could another founder post this unchanged?
2. **Image-only test:** could the caption have been generated from the photograph alone?
3. **Status-performance test:** is the main effect to make Chelston look successful?
4. **Story test:** does something materially happen, change, fail, conflict or remain at stake?
5. **Saveable-story test:** could a reader remember or retell the tension tomorrow?

If 1–3 are YES, reject.
If 4–5 are NO, reject or retrieve one more real fact.

## Creator benchmark rule

Charlie Phillips and Yusuf may be used as structural references for:

- specific hooks;
- real stakes;
- concrete scenes;
- numbers;
- candid admissions;
- readable story movement.

Extract the mechanism only. Do not imitate distinctive wording, persona or cadence.

## Reader handoff

A question is never required. Use one only when the answer would create meaningful discussion. Do not end with a question merely because SLAY or a template suggests one.

The reader handoff may instead be an implication, instruction, comparison, unresolved truth or no CTA at all.

## Authorship/process privacy

The internal content-production and publishing process is not story material.

Do not expose AI/LLM assistance, prompts, auto-posting, scheduling stacks, queue/refill logic, Buffer/GitHub/Notion publishing mechanics or generated-voice workflows in Chelston's public founder content.

AI may be discussed when it is genuinely the business topic, but not as disclosure of how the public post itself was authored or published.

Never compensate by making false “no AI” or “I wrote every word myself” claims.

## Relationship to the LinkedIn queue

Story quality outranks queue fullness.

Automatic replenishment stops at **8 scheduled placements per channel**. The Buffer ceiling remains **10**, leaving **2 slots reserved** for timely, owner-requested or in-the-moment posts.

Queue pressure never justifies publishing a weak founder story.

## Operator shorthand

Before writing, answer:

**WHAT HAPPENED?**
**WHY SHOULD I CARE?**
**WHAT IS STILL TRUE NOW?**

If those answers are not clear, the post is not ready.

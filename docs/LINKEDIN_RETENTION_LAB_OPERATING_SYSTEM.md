# 222 Emails | Retention Lab — LinkedIn Showcase Operating System

## Status
LOCKED operating direction for the existing secondary LinkedIn Showcase Page.

## Strategic role
The Showcase Page is **not** a duplicate company page.

It is the educational and diagnostic media arm of 222 Emails, dedicated to helping service businesses and other suitable SMEs understand where repeat revenue, follow-up and customer-return opportunities are being lost.

### Brand architecture
- **Chelston personal** — founder POV, lived lessons, conviction, commentary, story, relationships and high-trust authority.
- **222 Emails main** — company authority, services, proof, offers, outcomes, client stories, fit checks and conversion.
- **222 Emails | Retention Lab** — education, diagnostics, teardown-style thinking, experiments, frameworks, customer-journey maps, retention observations and actionable lessons.

## Page name
**222 Emails | Retention Lab**

Fallback if LinkedIn rejects punctuation or length:
**222 Emails Retention Lab**

## Tagline
**Where customer journeys get diagnosed before more money gets spent on leads.**

Alternative short tagline:
**Retention, follow-up and client-return systems for growing businesses.**

## About / description
222 Emails | Retention Lab is the educational arm of 222 Emails.

We break down the moments where good enquiries, first-time customers and existing clients quietly fall out of the journey — then show how better follow-up, retention logic and email systems can make the next step easier.

Expect practical diagnostics, client-return frameworks, teardown-style lessons, experiments and examples you can apply to your own business.

The goal is not more email for the sake of email.

The goal is fewer missed opportunities, more useful follow-up and a clearer route back for customers who already showed intent or trust.

Built by 222 Emails.

## Audience
Primary:
- salons, aesthetics, clinics and appointment-led businesses
- gyms, coaches and membership/service businesses
- professional services
- local and regional SMEs with repeat or follow-up revenue potential
- ecommerce brands where lifecycle/retention is material

Secondary:
- marketers and operators interested in retention systems
- agency partners and white-label partners
- founders who want practical customer-journey education

## Content promise
A follower should expect to become better at spotting and fixing lost-return opportunities.

Every post should answer at least one of these questions:
1. Where is revenue or customer intent leaking?
2. Why is it happening?
3. What should happen next?
4. What system, trigger or message could improve it?
5. How can the business tell whether the change worked?

## Core content pillars
### 1. Retention diagnostics — 30%
- five-point leak checks
- first-to-second purchase/appointment gaps
- lapsed-customer analysis
- enquiry and quote follow-up gaps
- quiet-week diagnosis

### 2. Customer journey education — 25%
- return-window mapping
- booking platform vs follow-up strategy
- newsletters vs behavioural journeys
- stop rules and exclusions
- segmentation by customer state

### 3. Teardowns and examples — 20%
- anonymised audits
- hypothetical before/after journeys
- good vs weak follow-up
- screenshots/flows where permission permits
- public examples with careful sourcing

### 4. Experiments and observations — 15%
- messaging tests
- CTA tests
- sequencing ideas
- timing hypotheses
- lessons from implementation

### 5. Soft conversion — 10%
- Fit Check invitation
- audit framework
- diagnostic checklist
- route to 222 Emails main Page / enquiry

## Funnel mix
- **TOF 55%** — sharp education, pattern recognition, diagnostic hooks.
- **MOF 35%** — frameworks, comparisons, examples, teardown logic.
- **BOF 10%** — Fit Check, audits, proof and clear invitations.

## Editorial rules
1. Do not mirror the main Page verbatim.
2. Do not post identical copy across Personal, Main and Retention Lab.
3. The Lab should teach before it sells.
4. Every commercial post needs a diagnostic or educational reason to exist.
5. Use problem/outcome language before mechanism language.
6. Prefer concrete customer moments over generic email-marketing advice.
7. No fabricated results, testimonials or case studies.
8. Claims requiring evidence must be sourced or framed as hypotheses/observations.
9. The page may reuse a master *concept*, but the execution must be rewritten for the Lab audience.
10. Retention Lab posts should usually stand alone even if the reader never visits the main Page.

## Posting logic
Target: up to **10 posts/day** when the approved master contains enough genuinely useful material.

High volume must be earned by variety. Avoid ten near-identical text posts.

Suggested daily mix at maximum cadence:
- 3 diagnostics / pattern-recognition posts
- 2 customer-journey lessons
- 2 examples / teardown-style posts
- 1 experiment / observation
- 1 concise framework/checklist
- 1 soft conversion / Fit Check post

The Buffer Free queue remains capped at 10 stored scheduled posts for this channel at a time. The capacity sweeper should refill the queue only from owner-approved placements.

## CTA hierarchy
Most posts: no CTA or a discussion CTA.

MOF CTAs:
- “Comment CHECK and I’ll send the framework.”
- “Which part of this journey leaks most often in your business?”
- “Save this before reviewing your follow-up.”

BOF CTAs:
- “Message FIT for the short qualification route.”
- “Visit 222 Emails if you want us to diagnose the journey with you.”

Avoid making every post a pitch.
